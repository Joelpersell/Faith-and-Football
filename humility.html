Humility: The Key to All Virtues

Joel Persell

July 30, 2026

Humility is one of those qualities everyone admires and respects. It is what makes Messi not just the greatest footballer, but a role model for young men. We all know, almost by instinct, that a humble attitude is superior to a prideful one, even if we struggle to live that out on a daily basis. This is only emphasized for followers of Christ, as the God-Man is the perfect embodiment of humility. However, what if I were to tell you that humility is more than just another virtue, but rather the root of all other virtues? In other words, we cannot hope to achieve any virtue at all, unless we first begin with a humble heart.

At first glance, this may seem like a stretch. In fact, you might even know people who seem to lack humility, while nevertheless exemplifying other virtues. Would this not invalidate my case? In response, I would say that no one—other than Christ—has ever achieved perfect humility in this life. Furthermore, ‘perfect’ humility is not required before other virtues can be pursued. If it were, no one would ever attain any virtue whatsoever. The truth is, all of us have a certain degree of humility, and a certain degree of pride. One is the root of all virtue; the other is the root of all evil. Therefore, the level of virtue or vice we display will correspond to the level of humility or pride we possess.

Since I plan to write a whole separate article on pride, I will keep the focus primarily on humility for the time being. Still, it helps to remember that the two are diametrically opposed to one another, and as a result of this, it is difficult to talk about one without mentioning the other. Naturally, then, it will help to define both humility and pride before delving any deeper.

We have all likely heard the common definitions of pride and humility before, perhaps more times than we can count. Pride is having a high admiration of oneself, while humility is quite simply the opposite—having a modest view of oneself. There is nothing wrong with those definitions, but I think the following definitions will serve us slightly better. Humility is a focus on others, even at the expense of oneself. Conversely, pride is a focus on oneself, even at the expense of others.

What this all boils down to is one simple thought—it’s not about me. The importance of this concept cannot be overstated. Of course, I realize I am taking for granted the Christian faith and tradition. Not all religious systems would place the same emphasis on humility. Unavoidably, all of my articles will contain this bias, since I believe that Jesus died and rose again, ascending to sit at the right hand of God the Father until he returns to judge the living and the dead. So, I will now proceed to demonstrate the centrality of humility in the Christian system, using none other than Jesus himself.

When Paul says, “Do nothing from selfish ambition or conceit, but in humility count others more significant than yourselves,” he goes on to use Jesus as the prime example of this mindset (Philippians‬ ‭2‬:‭3‬, ESV‬‬). The climax of the example is this: “And being found in human form, he humbled himself by becoming obedient to the point of death, even death on a cross” (Philippians‬ ‭2‬:‭8‬, ESV‬‬). If God willingly took on flesh and allowed himself to be crucified for us, then what is our excuse for putting ourselves before others? The point is painfully clear.

Hear also the words of Jesus, as he summarizes the whole Law in just a few sentences: “And he said to him, ‘You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind. This is the great and first commandment. And a second is like it: You shall love your neighbor as yourself. On these two commandments depend all the Law and the Prophets’” (Matthew‬ ‭22‬:‭37‬-‭40‬, ESV‬). According to Jesus, it’s all about love. So the question is this: how do we learn to love?

Now, with this in front of us, we can begin to look at how humility is the key to all virtues, including the big theological ones, like faith, hope, and yes, even love. You see, neither faith, hope, nor love can exist where there is pride. Only by setting our focus outside of ourselves can we make room for trust in our hearts, which is the foundation of both faith and hope. Moreover, if we aim to put our faith and hope in God, an even greater deal of trust is required, since we cannot yet see him face to face. And how can we trust God at all, if we do not first admit our own shortcomings?

We must start by recognizing the reality that we cannot save ourselves—death comes for us all. Once we do that, we are open to trusting someone else—someone capable of overcoming death. That is the beginning of true humility. This childlike dependence is what leads to faith in God and hope in his salvation. That seems to be exactly what Jesus was getting at when he said, “Truly, I say to you, unless you turn and become like children, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven. Whoever humbles himself like this child is the greatest in the kingdom of heaven” (Matthew‬ ‭18‬:‭3‬-‭4‬, ESV‬).

So we see how faith and hope—built on the foundation of trust—are rooted in humility. But what about love? Without a doubt, love is no less dependent upon humility than faith or hope. In fact, it is perhaps even more tightly bound to humility than the others. Love simply cannot exist apart from humility. If we are too busy focusing on ourselves, we will not be able to love God, much less our neighbors. And since love is the foundation of “all the Law and the Prophets,” we effectively kill any possibility of living a moral life, simply by neglecting humility.

This intricate connection between love and humility is fascinating. At the most basic level, both are about putting others before yourself. Although we often think of love in terms of affection, real biblical love is almost indistinguishable from humility. It is Paul’s call to “Do nothing from selfish ambition or conceit, but in humility count others more significant than yourselves” (Philippians 2:3, ESV). That is how God showed us his love at the cross. He counted our salvation as more important than his own avoidance of suffering.

At this point, you should be able to see how humility is connected to the other virtues as well, without me having to go through each and every one of them. If you want to test it for yourself, just start by going through the fruits of the Spirit in Galatians 5:22-23. By focusing solely on yourself, you will never bear a single fruit on that list. On the other hand, if your focus is on God and others, these fruits of the Spirit—and all other godly virtues—will begin to fall into place one by one.

So, if you want to live a life of virtue, pray for a humble heart. Start each day with a delightfully simple thought in the forefront of your mind: “it’s not about me.” Forget about yourself and what you want. Instead, focus on God and what he wants; on your neighbor and what he needs. Only then can we begin to obey the Law and the Prophets. Only then can we learn to love God and neighbor. Otherwise, all virtues will remain hidden behind a locked door.

The path to God may not be easy, but it is clear and simple. The door is Christ. The cross-shaped key is humility. God help us surrender our lives and follow in his perfect footsteps, “saying, ‘Father, if you are willing, remove this cup from me. Nevertheless, not my will, but yours, be done’” (Luke‬ ‭22‬:‭42‬, ESV‬‬).
